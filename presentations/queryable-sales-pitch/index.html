⏱ 2 to 3 minutes. One card at a time, in reading order.

Meetings: the bot joins on its own, from the calendar, a few minutes before the start time. It records, transcribes with each speaker's name and delivers summaries, decisions and tasks.

Conversations: WhatsApp, Slack and Discord groups. Each person's Gmail is searched live, without being copied.

Documents: Drive, OneDrive, Notion, GitHub, Zendesk. What changes there changes here.

Systems: the company connects its databases, Postgres or BigQuery, and chooses which tables the agent can see. People ask in plain language; the agent writes a read-only query, answers and can draw a chart. The CRM is queried when the question is asked.

Agents: questions in plain language, agents called with @ and the company's Claude connected through MCP. Tasks go to ClickUp or Jira.

Specialized agents: Queryable comes with agents for specific jobs. In sales, they score each sales call against a scorecard and track objections and signals. In onboarding, they put together a plan for each new hire. In support, they work with the support team's history.

The idea. What happens to each source when it comes into Queryable, and the agents that work on top of it.

On screen. Six cards: meetings, conversations, documents and systems in gray, like the sources in the drawings; agents and specialized agents in orange, like the agents.

Detail. Gmail and the CRM are queried live (they are not copied into memory). Database connections are read-only and go through a short onboarding (tables, context questions, KPIs). The full list of integrations is on the hidden backup slide near the end (open the deck with ?all).

02 · With Queryable

Each person and each agent sees only what they can see.

  • Owners and admins see the whole organization. Members see only the meetings they took part in.
  • Private channels and DMs never become memory. Not even for admins.
  • An agent never sees more than the person asking. It inherits the limits of whoever called it.
  • Outside tools come in read-only. Writing has to be enabled.

⏱ 1 to 2 minutes. This is the question that always comes up: what about security?

First rule: owners and admins see the organization. A member sees the meetings they took part in.

Second: private channels and direct messages don't go into memory, not even for the admin.

Third: an agent answers with at most the access of the person who asked. No one can use the agent to see what they couldn't see themselves.

Fourth: Claude or any other tool connected through MCP comes in read-only, unless the company enables writing.

The idea. Having everything in one place only works if access is respected. Four product rules: the role defines what each person sees, private conversations stay out of memory, the agent inherits the limits of whoever asks and outside connections start read-only.

On screen. The opening sentence and the four rules.

If asked. Each organization has its own data, separated by email domain. The MCP token has read scope by default; write scope has to be chosen.

03 · Queryable 2.0

⏱ 2 minutes. Make it clear that this is where the product is heading.

In the third scenario, Queryable 2.0, the tools no longer sit outside sending data in. The meeting, the conversation, the document and the system happen inside Queryable. There is no integration to maintain: what happens here is already memory.

At the center, agents and memory are one thing. The agent answers, acts and serves, always under the same access control.

Below, people work in one place.

And customers can talk to a person or straight to the agents, who already know their history.

The idea. Scenario 3, Queryable 2.0: where the product is heading. Meetings, conversations, documents and systems (CRM, ERP, accounting) become Queryable modules, and the central block brings agents and memory together. The outside world comes in through people or straight through the agents.

On screen. Queryable 2.0 with the four modules, the central block, people and the outside world, with both paths (through people, and straight to the agents). Same colors as slides 3 and 4: agents in orange, people in blue, the rest in gray.

For the presenter. Today the native parts are channels, DMs and projects with tasks. In-platform calls, a document editor and ERP and accounting modules don't exist yet. Present it as a direction, not as a finished product.
