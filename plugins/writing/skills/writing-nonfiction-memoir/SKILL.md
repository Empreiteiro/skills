# Non-fiction & Memoir

The reader of true writing signs a contract: everything here happened, and the narrator knows the difference
between what she remembers, what she has on record and what she has come to think. Inside that contract the
reader wants a particular life or a particular piece of the world, and a narrator who has thought about it.
What goes wrong most often is smoothing: an edit that cleans a quote, fills a gap with a plausible detail, or
trades the writer's specific, uncertain memory for a confident sentence that could belong to anyone.

## The standards

Items marked *(core)* are the short list to hand a writer or a model; the rest are the wider craft.

1. **The specific is the point** *(core)*. Names, dates, places, brand names and the odd detail are why
   the reader came. Portability test: a sentence that could sit unchanged in anyone's book says nothing about
   this one; anchor it to something already in the draft or cut it.
2. **Nothing enters that was not there** *(core)*. No memory, quote or date, and none of the small
   "safe" additions either: weather, light, clothing, a day of the week, *several* turned into *six*, *in the
   spring* turned into *a Tuesday in April*. McPhee: creative nonfiction is making the most of what you have,
   not making something up.
3. **Cut the moral; keep the realization** *(core)*. A sentence that tells the reader what to
   feel or what life teaches goes. What the narrator reports feeling or understanding at the time is an event in
   the story and stays.
   - Cut: *Sometimes the people who love us most are the ones who hurt us.*
   - Keep: *I understood, holding her coat, that she was not coming back for it.*
4. **Keep the two selves apart.** Memoir has a narrator now and a self then (Birkerts). The younger self does
   not know what the narrator knows; hindsight is either marked (*I know now*, *years later*) or withheld until
   the point in the story where it arrived. No one in a reported scene acts on what they learned afterwards.
5. **Reflection has to think.** Lopate's point: the nonfiction narrator is also there to tell, to argue with
   herself on the page. A reflective passage earns its place when it complicates, doubts or finds a connection
   the scene could not; it fails when it summarizes the scene or arrives at wisdom before the friction has.
6. **Uncertainty stays uncertain, and certainty stays certain.** *I think it was March* keeps its *I think*;
   Karr's argument is that enacting memory's blur is what earns the reader's trust. The reverse holds too: never
   add a hedge to something the writer states as known.
7. **No edit raises a claim's strength.** *May* stays *may*; *was associated with* does not become *caused*;
   *about a third* does not become *33%*; attribution, scope and dates are not dropped to tighten a sentence.
   Only new evidence strengthens a claim, and an edit has none (lyndonkl's claim-strength invariant).
8. **Scene for what mattered, summary for the rest.** The moments that changed the writer or the story are shown
   happening, in time, with what was said and done (Gutkind's building blocks; Wolfe's scene-by-scene). A scene
   can only hold what the writer remembers or has on record; when that is too thin, it becomes summary, never a
   scene padded with invention.
9. **Quotes are verbatim, and speech keeps its texture.** Grammar slips, dialect, repetition and false starts in
   what someone said stay. A quotation that lands the chapter's theme in one polished sentence is a warning
   sign, not a gift.
10. **The narrator's own part stays in.** Gornick's truth-speaking narrator owns her part: the fear, the
    self-pity, the complicity. An edit that makes the narrator kinder, braver or more right than the draft did
    makes the book false, and so does one that makes anyone else crueler.
11. **Two people stay two people; two events stay two events.** No composite characters, no merging of separate
    conversations into one scene, no compressed timeline the writer did not choose and disclose.
12. **Honest negativity is a feature.** A hardship does not have to end in growth, and a judgment can stay mixed.
    A relentless redemption arc reads as manufactured, and positivity on every page reads as a machine.
13. **Every number brings its source and its scale.** Who measured it, when, and compared with what. *Studies
    show* is not a source; when the draft gives none, the gap is the writer's to fill.
14. **Arrive instead of announcing.** In trade nonfiction, no *In this chapter we will explore* and no recap at
    the end of a chapter that argued its point well; start with the case and end on the last concrete point.

## How a piece is built

**Before drafting.** Gornick separates the situation (the circumstance, sometimes the plot) from the story (the
thing the writer has come to say). Write the story in one sentence first. Decide who is speaking: the narrator
at what age, from what vantage, with what relation to the events. Then list what is actually remembered or on
record, scene by scene; a gap on that list is a sentence like *I don't remember whether it rained* or a question
for the writer, never a slot for the AI to fill.

**The shapes this craft covers.**

| Form | Its spine | What the reader came for | The usual way it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Memoir | A question about the writer's life, asked from a vantage | A particular life and a narrator who has thought about it | The moral, the tidy redemption, the invented scene |
| Personal narrative (a chapter or shorter piece) | One experience and what it opened | A turn in understanding | Wisdom arriving before the friction |
| Narrative nonfiction, story form | Real people, a complication, a resolution (Hart) | Being inside events that happened | Invented interiority; hindsight in the scene |
| Explanatory narrative | A narrative line with digressions into context (McPhee, Orlean) | Understanding a world through one story | Digressions that bury the line |
| Biography and history | A life or a period on the record | A fair, sourced account | False precision, composite scenes, judging the past by later terms |
| Idea-driven trade nonfiction | One argument, chapter by chapter, carried by cases | An idea they can use, earned by evidence | Signposting, recap, *studies show*, overclaiming |

**Openings.** Open in a moment: a person, a place, something happening. An opening that generalizes about the
subject, addresses an abstract *you* or states the book's meaning before any scene has earned it reads as an
essay (book-editor's opening diagnostic). The first sentence where the writing comes alive in concrete detail
is usually the real beginning. In trade nonfiction the equivalent is a case, a problem or a number with its
source, not *In today's world*.

**Middles** alternate scene and reflection, and the proportion is a choice. Gutkind's yellow test (highlight
the scenes and look at the ratio) shows whether a chapter is mostly report. Chronology is optional: McPhee
builds the structure first and lets the order of telling serve the material, and memoir often enters at the
moment of highest charge and loops back.

**Endings** are short. Let a chapter end on its last concrete point or image; cut the coda that explains what
it all meant and the paragraph that restates the thesis. A memoir may end without resolution if the life has
not resolved.

**Truth conventions** (changed names, reconstructed dialogue, compressed time) are the writer's decisions and
belong in an author's note the writer writes. Karr accepts reconstructed dialogue and changed names and rejects
invented events; the AI never adds a disclaimer or decides which convention applies.

## The editing pass

- [ ] Every name, date, place, number and quoted word in the passage is identical after the edit.
- [ ] Nothing entered: no sensory detail, quote, figure, date, event or person that was not in the draft.
- [ ] Each claim kept its strength: hedges, attributions, scope words and dates are intact, and no hedge was
      added to a fact the writer states plainly.
- [ ] Quotes are verbatim, grammar and dialect included.
- [ ] After each scene: a moral addressed to the reader is cut; the narrator's realization at the time is kept.
- [ ] Hindsight is marked or withheld; the younger self does not know what the narrator knows now.
- [ ] Where the writer was unsure, the edit is still unsure.
- [ ] Portability test passed: no sentence could be dropped into someone else's book unchanged.
- [ ] The narrator's unflattering moments and mixed judgments survived.
- [ ] The opening is a moment (or a case); the ending is the last concrete point, not a recap.
- [ ] Trade nonfiction: no announcement of what the chapter will do, every statistic has a source, every
      citation marker still sits next to the claim it supports.
- [ ] Tells from "What goes wrong" do not cluster in the passage.

## What goes wrong

| Pattern | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The prophecy | *Little did I know that summer would change everything.* | Cut it; let the change arrive where it happened. |
| The rear-view mirror | *Looking back, I realize…* opening every reflection | The realization stays; the repeated framing goes when a note asks to tighten. |
| The moral coda | *Grief, I have learned, has no timetable.* | End on the scene. |
| The lesson list | *That year taught me three things: patience, humility and trust.* | Cut the list; the chapter already showed whatever it showed. |
| Thesis leakage | A sentence that explains the image just before it | Cut it, or move the interpretation into a concrete image (book-editor). |
| Wisdom too soon | Peace and insight in paragraph two of a hard chapter | Delay it until the friction has been felt. |
| Sacred inflation | *Sacred*, *energy*, *journey*, *healing* before the reader knows why the moment matters | Ground it in the room, the body, the object; keep the word only if the writer uses it. |
| The invented sense | *The kitchen smelled of cinnamon that morning* where the draft had no smell | Never add it. Thin is honest. |
| The too-clean quote | A parent who speaks in polished aphorisms that land the theme | Restore the writer's version; never polish speech. |
| The undocumented turn | *By then it was clear…*, *the mood had shifted*, *now convinced* | Keep only what the draft supports; this is where invention hides (lyndonkl). |
| The causal seam | *which led to*, *forced*, *triggered* placed between two facts the draft only set side by side | Restore the writer's sequence; adjacency is not cause. |
| Over-hedging | *I think maybe it was perhaps around then* on every line | Do not add hedges; if a note asks to cut, keep the ones that mark real doubt. |
| The universal *we* | *We all know what it is like to lose someone.* | The particular person, the particular loss. |
| Signposting | *In this chapter, we'll explore three ways…* | Start with the case. |
| The redemption default | Every hardship resolved into growth by the last paragraph | Leave the ending where the writer left it. |
| Machine register | *A testament to*, *navigating*, *a rich tapestry*, *not just X but Y*, tidy triads | Say the plain thing in the writer's words. |

## When the AI edits someone else's draft

- Freeze the facts. Every name, date, number, place and quoted word in the marked passage comes out identical,
  unless the note itself supplies the corrected value.
- Add nothing, however small or safe it looks. When the note asks for something only the writer knows (who,
  when, what was said, what happened next), leave the passage exactly as written.
- What the writer reports feeling, perceiving, deciding or understanding is theirs. Do not cut it, recast it as
  behavior, or turn *I realized that X* into a bare *X* unless the note asks (sepia's memoir guardrail). The
  reflective sentence to cut is the moral addressed to the reader, not the narrator's experience.
- Keep the writer's hedges and uncertainty markers, and never insert new ones around facts stated plainly.
- Quotation marks are a promise. Never put them around a paraphrase, never remove them from a quote, never
  edit inside them. Keep the writer's convention for remembered speech (quotes, italics or none).
- Do not change who the narrator is: the narrating tense and vantage, their opinions, their fairness or
  unfairness. Nobody in the passage comes out kinder or crueler than the draft made them.
- Leave changed names, initials, redactions, footnote markers and citations exactly where they are; a
  compression that separates a claim from its citation breaks it.

## Standing instructions

Short lines a writer can keep in their own brief or style notes, so that an assistant applies them every
time. Each one is a habit drawn from the standards above, phrased in the writer's voice; keep the few that
fit the project.

- Never upgrade a claim: "may" stays "may" and "about a third" stays "about a third".
- No "little did I know" and no "looking back, I realize".
- Quote people exactly, bad grammar and dialect included.
- Let the younger self not know what the narrator knows now.
- Leave in the details that make the narrator look bad.
- Never merge two people or two events into one to tidy the story.
- Give every statistic its source and its year.
- Open chapters on a moment, not on background or a thesis.
