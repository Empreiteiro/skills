# Social Media

A post is read by a thumb in motion: someone deciding from the first line whether to stop, and,
further down, the people who already follow the writer and know how they sound. They need one idea
worth the stop, said the way this writer says things, in the platform's native shape. What goes wrong
most is the post that sounds like a brand account: smoothed, padded into a thread or into one-sentence
lines, and closed with a question nobody wants to answer.

## The standards

1. **Get the claim in before the cut.** Each platform hides the rest of a post at a different point.
   A standard X post has no fold, so its first line carries everything; LinkedIn cuts at about 140 characters on a
   phone and 210 on desktop, and every blank line counts as a line; an Instagram caption cuts at
   about 125; Threads after a line or two. What must be read goes above that point, and the topic is
   named in the opening words so the reader knows what the post is about before deciding.
   - Before: "I've been thinking a lot lately about hiring, and something surprised me."
   - After: "We hired four engineers this year without one whiteboard interview. Each finalist got paid
     for a day of real work instead."
2. **Open on something only this writer could say.** A number with a referent, a named tool or
   person, a scene, a fact that costs the writer something to admit. Not a sincerity announcement
   ("Let me be honest", "Unpopular opinion:" on a popular take) and not a hook template with the
   blanks filled in; performed candour is now one of the surest signs of a generated post.
3. **A thread is one idea in steps, and every post in it stands alone.** Readers land on post four
   from a quote or a repost, so each post must make sense without the others. If the idea fits in one
   post, it is one post; padding a single line into a thread is the commonest way threads fail.
4. **Write for the send, not the like.** Ask of every post: would someone forward this to one
   particular person? X's open-sourced ranker scores replies, shares, follows and profile clicks
   separately from likes, and LinkedIn's reported shift puts saves and reading time above
   reactions. Useful, specific or surprising earns those; agreeable does not.
5. **One real number or name per post, never an invented one.** A specific beats an adjective
   ("2.4x", "Stripe", "last Tuesday"), but a wrong number on a public feed becomes someone else's
   quote post. Every figure, client and date comes from the writer.
6. **Line breaks are punctuation; put them where the writer pauses.** One sentence per line all the
   way down ("broetry") reads as a template, and a twelve-line paragraph loses a phone reader. Break
   between beats, keep sentences that belong together together, and follow the writer's own habit
   when their past posts show one.
7. **End on the point.** The last line is the last concrete thing, a question the writer genuinely
   wants answered, or a clean stop. Not a recap, not a fake-profound kicker ("And that changes
   everything."), not "Follow for more" stapled to every post.
8. **Stay inside the hard limits, counted.** X allows 280 weighted characters (a link counts as 23,
   an emoji or a CJK character as 2; Premium allows 25,000). Threads allows 500 per post and one topic
   tag. Bluesky allows 300 graphemes. LinkedIn allows 3,000. An Instagram caption allows 2,200 and,
   since December 2025, five hashtags. A post that runs to 290 needs an edit, not a second post.
9. **Repurposing changes the delivery, never the claim.** Moving a post across platforms means a new
   first line for the new fold, a cut (LinkedIn to X usually loses around 40%), and stripping the old
   platform's furniture: "link in bio" where links are clickable, hashtag walls, "as I posted on
   LinkedIn". Facts, numbers and quotes stay identical.
10. **Use hashtags as labels, and few of them.** Zero or one on X, at most one topic tag on Threads, at most
    five on Instagram (the platform's own cap), few or none on LinkedIn. They go at the end, written
    in CamelCase (#RemoteWork, not #remotework) so a screen reader can pronounce them.
11. **An emoji is read aloud as a word.** A screen reader reads each one aloud ("rocket, check mark, light
    bulb"). Zero or one per post unless the writer's own posts use more; never as list markers and
    never on every line.
12. **Announce the news, not the feeling.** "We raised $4M to hire our first five engineers" opens
    the post; "I'm humbled and thrilled to share" is cut. No manufactured vulnerability, no
    humblebrag frame, no lesson bolted onto an event that didn't teach one.
13. **Keep the register the writer posts in.** Lowercase-casual on X and Threads is a real register,
    and so are full sentences on LinkedIn; don't capitalise the first into a brand voice or lowercase
    the second to sound native. Names, products and companies keep their capitals either way.
14. **Replies and quote posts add one thing.** A fact, an example, a counter-case or a correction, in
    the first sentence. Never "Great post!", a restatement of the original, or a pitch for the
    writer's product under someone else's post.
15. **Every image carries alt text.** Say what the image shows and why it is in the post; text inside
    an image (a screenshot, a quote card, a chart's numbers) is repeated in full in the alt text.

## How a piece is built

**Before drafting**, settle five things: the platform; the goal (replies, saves, shares, follows or a
click); the one idea, written as a single sentence; the specific that carries it (a number, a moment,
an example); and the writer's last five to ten posts on that platform, which are the voice reference.
When the writer has no posts, take the voice from their other writing and say so.

**The platforms differ in more than length.**

| Platform | Hard limit | Visible before the cut | Native register | Tags |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| X | 280 weighted (25,000 on Premium) | Whole post; no fold | Compressed, often lowercase, one beat per line | 0–1, at the end |
| LinkedIn | 3,000 | About 140 characters on a phone, 210 on desktop, or 3 lines | Full sentences; a story with what it taught | 0–3, at the end |
| Instagram caption | 2,200 | About 125 characters | Serves the image or video; warmer | Up to 5 (platform cap) |
| Threads | 500 per post | A line or two | Conversational, warmer than X; questions get real answers | 1 topic tag at most |
| Bluesky | 300 graphemes | Whole post | Plain and conversational | Few |

None of these feeds renders Markdown: `**bold**` arrives as asterisks and `#` headings as stray symbols.

**Shapes.**

| Shape | Built as |
|---|---|
| A take | The claim in line one → the one reason or example behind it → stop |
| A story | The moment in line one → what happened → what changed → the one thing a reader can use; no moral stapled on |
| Thread or carousel | Post or slide 1 states the payoff (and the count, if there is one) → one step per post, each able to stand alone → the last one holds the most quotable line or the resource |
| Announcement | The news, with the number or the name → why it matters to the reader → the next step or link |
| Caption | What the image can't say: the context, the number, the why; the first ~125 characters work alone |
| Reply or quote post | The one added thing, in the first sentence |

**Openings** name the topic and land a specific in the first eight to ten words. **Closings** stop
when the point is made; a question is allowed when the writer would read every answer.

## The editing pass

- [ ] Read alone, the first line says what the post is about and gives a reason to keep reading.
- [ ] Everything above the platform's cut works without the rest.
- [ ] One idea; a second idea has been cut or saved for its own post.
- [ ] Counted against the platform's limit (weighted on X, graphemes on Bluesky), and the count fits.
- [ ] Every number, name and date is real and came from the writer.
- [ ] No engagement bait, no "thread 🧵" opener, no "Let that sink in", "Read that again" or "Agree?".
- [ ] No sincerity announcement, humblebrag frame or bolted-on lesson.
- [ ] No contrast reveal, negation list, self-answered question or staccato drumbeat ("Short. Punchy. Done.").
- [ ] Line breaks fall on the beats; neither broetry nor a wall.
- [ ] Hashtags within the platform's count, at the end, in CamelCase.
- [ ] Emoji at the writer's own rate, none as bullets.
- [ ] Each thread post stands alone, and the last one is worth reposting.
- [ ] Every image has alt text, and text inside images is repeated there.
- [ ] Next to the writer's last ten posts, it sounds like the same person, not like a brand account.

## What goes wrong

| Pattern | Looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Thread throat-clearing | "A thread on everything I learned about pricing (1/14)", spool emoji included | Start with the first lesson; the format announces itself |
| Secret framing | "Here's what nobody tells you about fundraising:" | Say the thing; if it's genuinely little-known, the content shows it |
| Feelings-first announcement | "I'm humbled and excited to announce…" | The news, with its number or name, in line one |
| Fake contrarian | "Unpopular opinion: you should talk to your customers." | State it flat, or find the part that really is contested |
| Obituary hook | "Cold e-mail is dead." | Say what changed and the number that shows it |
| Inflated listicle | "7 lessons from my first year as a founder" with one real lesson | Write the one lesson, well |
| Broetry | One short sentence per line for thirty lines | Paragraphs that break where a person would pause |
| Emoji scaffolding | A rocket, a check mark or a light bulb leading every line | Plain lines, or one emoji where it carries meaning |
| Dead closer | "Agree?", "Thoughts?", "Comment YES if…", "Let that sink in." | End on the last concrete line, or a question the writer wants answered |
| Reveal bridges | "The result? 3x more replies." / "The best part:" | Write the sentence: "Replies tripled in two weeks." |
| Credential opener | "As a CMO with 15 years of experience…" | Let the experience show in the detail of the post |
| Hashtag wall | Twelve tags under a LinkedIn post | The platform's count at most, or none |
| Repurposing scars | "Link in bio" on X, "(1/12)" on a post that fits in one, "as I wrote on LinkedIn" | Rebuild for the new platform; never admit the copy-paste |
| Over-correction | A humanizing pass that adds slang, lowercase or typos the writer never uses | Take casual style only from the writer's own posts |

## When the AI edits someone else's draft

- Keep line breaks, blank lines, capitalisation (lowercase sentence starts included), emoji,
  @mentions, hashtags, links and thread numbering ("1/", "2/") exactly where the writer put them,
  unless the comment is about them.
- The rewrite fits the same platform limit as the original, counted the platform's way; a rewritten
  first line still lands its claim above the fold.
- Keep bluntness, profanity, slang and in-jokes at full strength. Add no hedge, no balance and no
  softer verb.
- Never add a hashtag, an emoji, a call to action, a closing question or a "follow for more" the
  writer didn't write, and never introduce Markdown the platform will print as symbols.
- Numbers, names, quotes and the text of screenshots stay untouched; a rewrite adds no new claim.
- In a thread, rewrite only the marked post. Don't merge, split or renumber posts unless asked, and
  keep each one able to stand alone.
- Take the voice from the writer's unmarked posts around the passage, not from platform templates
  or hook formulas.

## Standing instructions

Short lines a writer can keep in their own brief or style notes, so that an assistant applies them every
time. Each one is a habit drawn from the standards above, phrased in the writer's voice; keep the few that
fit the project.

- Use one real number or name in every post where the claim allows it.
- Break lines where I'd pause, not after every sentence.
- Count characters against the platform's limit before handing a post back.
- At most one emoji per post, and never as a bullet.
- Every post in a thread must make sense read on its own.
- Announce the news itself in the first line, not how I feel about it.
- Never open with "Unpopular opinion", "Hot take" or "Let me be honest".
- Write hashtags in CamelCase so screen readers can say them.
