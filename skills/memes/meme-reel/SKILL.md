# Meme reel

Reels at **1080x1920, 30 fps**, generated by code. There is no timeline and no After Effects: a scene is a
function that receives the frame number and draws it, and frames are piped straight into ffmpeg.

```
scripts/stage.py          the 9:16 frame, the timing curves, the post, the encoder (Pillow + ffmpeg)
scripts/example_reel.py   an example scene: it rains paper bills, and the reel ends on the post
```

```bash
python3 ${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/example_reel.py rain.mp4 spec.json
```

`spec.json` is meme-post's card spec (`text`, `brand`, `handle`, `logo`, `theme`, `font`) plus `name`, `caption`
and `duration`. The post itself is drawn by meme-post's `card.py`, imported — the video must not become another
copy of the post's numbers. Needs `pip install pillow` and an ffmpeg (on the PATH, or `pip install imageio-ffmpeg`).

## The rule that applies to all of them

**Every reel ends on the usual art.** The last second of any of them is a post from the page, still — same header,
same margin, same font, the line on the same lines. The movement is only the entrance; what stays on the retina at
the end is the post.

This is not style. It is what makes different animations recognizable as coming from the same page, and it means
every reel doubles as its post. Break the rule only when the video is **not** a post — a loop made to put something
on top of, for instance.

The geometry follows from that: the square 1080 card sits **centered** in the vertical frame (`Y0 = 420`), and
the movement happens in the bands above and below. Since the frame's background is the card's background, there is
no seam. `example_reel.py` reports whether the last frame, cropped, is pixel-identical to the card.

## Where nothing may sit

The Reels interface covers **220px at the top and 420px at the bottom** (account name on top; caption, audio and
buttons below). The centered card is born inside (420 to 1500); what has to watch these numbers is whatever the
scene puts **outside** it — a stack, a grid, a floor. Measure from `SAFE_TOP` and `SAFE_BOTTOM` in `stage.py`; do
not guess a y.

| where | y in the frame | y on the square card |
|---|---|---|
| top of the safe area | 220 | — |
| top of the card | 420 | 0 |
| the header | 825 | 405 |
| the text | 1009 | 589 |
| bottom of the safe area | 1500 | 1080 |

## Timing

Everything in seconds, converted by `segment(f, start, end)`, which returns 0 to 1. On top of that:

| curve | shape | when |
|---|---|---|
| `smooth` | eases in and out | the default, for appearing and disappearing |
| `ease_out` | comes in fast and brakes | something that arrives with weight |
| `spring` | overshoots and comes back | a stamp, a token settling into place |

A tempo that works:

```
0.15 – 0.95   the header prints (the logo uncovers from left to right)
0.55 – 1.15   the page's name appears
1.00 – 1.80   the first line of the joke
   …          the movement
~5.5 – 6.5    the second line: the punchline
   end        ~1s still on the art, nothing moving
```

**The last second has to be still.** If something is still moving when the video ends, the platform loops it in
the middle of a gesture. Work out when the last thing stops and set the duration above that — `example_reel.py`
warns when less than a second is left.

## Writing a new scene

1. **The joke first.** The line and the caption come from [meme-post](../meme-post/SKILL.md): the reel does not
   change what the page says, it changes how it enters.
2. **The movement has to be the joke, not decoration.** If the animation could be swapped for another without the
   joke changing, it is not helping. In the example what falls is what the line says fell; a price could crack into
   twelve installments; a stack of bills could grow until it covers the frame.
3. **Copy `example_reel.py`.** Keep the frame loop and the closing on the card; change the movement. Keep the
   timing marks together at the top, as `T_*` constants, so the rhythm can be tuned without hunting numbers.
4. **Draw once, reuse.** Sprites are made once, outside the loop; rotations go through `Rotator`, which rounds
   the angle to 4 degrees and caches it. Nobody sees the step at 30 fps, and the render drops from minutes to
   seconds.
5. **Seed every random choice** with `random.Random(seed(name))`. The name gives a stable seed: same name, same
   video, forever — and the file is named after the scene (`rain.mp4`), not after a number.

```python
def scene(film, spec):
    for f in range(round(spec['duration'] * FPS)):
        img = frame(spec)
        # ... the movement, as a function of segment(f, a, b) ...
        post(img, spec,
             header_reveal=ease_out(segment(f, 0.15, 0.95)),
             header_alpha=smooth(segment(f, 0.55, 1.15)),
             line_alphas=[smooth(segment(f, 1.0, 1.8)), smooth(segment(f, 5.5, 6.2))])
        film.add(img)
```

`post()` takes one alpha per line of text as the card wraps it (`body_lines(spec)` shows the real breaks), and a
`drawing` alpha for chart posts. Anything drawn before `post()` goes behind the text; anything after, in front.

## Before calling it done

- [ ] The last second is **still**, on the page's art (`ends on the card: True`).
- [ ] Nothing that matters goes below y=1500 or above y=220.
- [ ] The text fits: about 34 characters per line in a monospace (`body_lines()` shows the real breaks).
- [ ] The MP4 opens, has an audio track and stays around 5 MB or less. Without an audio track some apps refuse the
      file on upload; if a scene bloats the file, raise its `crf` (`Film(path, crf=21)`) instead of lowering the
      quality of every scene.
- [ ] It ran twice and came out **byte-for-byte identical** (`sha256sum`). A reel is a function of the frame: if one
      render differs from another, unseeded randomness got in, and the video is no longer reproducible.
- [ ] The caption and the hashtag block are written (meme-post).

## Costs

| scene | render | file |
|---|---|---|
| the example rain, 36 sheets, 8 s | ~13 s | ~2.7 MB |
| a 1-bit game level (meme-game), 8.5 s | ~16 s | ~0.1 MB |

Most of the cost is compositing sprites; flat, two-color scenes compress to almost nothing. Fine grain and noise are
the worst case for H.264 — every pixel changes every frame — so if a scene needs grain, make it coarse.
